Finding one

The head-start fallacy

The notebook scores a day as a win when the 16:15 close is past the near-money strike in the predicted direction. That strike sits at most 12 points from the open. But the "strong drift" filter only fires when price has already moved more than 0.30% from the open by 10:00 — which on every one of those 64 days means price is already past the strike, by a median of 20 points. A "win" then only requires that price not give the whole head start back.

Two smaller things in the same vein

  • "Initial direction" was a one-second tick. The day's direction comes from the first bar whose open differs from the 09:30 open — the 09:30:01 tick on 432 days. It agrees with the close direction 55.6% of the time. The 09:35 direction agrees 57.7%: the market continues its first five minutes slightly more often than it reverses, so the premise is mildly wrong-signed. Neither is tradeable from 10:00.
  • The calendar filters were mildly lucky. Randomly relabeling the 127 wide-and-reversal days produces a subset at or above 76.9% (N ≥ 35) about 4% of the time under the 24 filters actually searched.

Finding two

What a mirage looks like in your own data

One new candidate came out of the exploration: at 10:00, trade against the overnight gap, hold to the close, no stop. On the ES sample it earned +5.4 points a day at t = 2.8, was positive in every year and both halves, survived removing the April 2025 tariff week, and passed a sign-flip permutation test across an 18-member threshold family at p below 0.01. Net of costs one MES contract came to about $5,300 a year against a $1,700 drawdown. Every test the notebook knows how to run said yes.

The same rule needs only the open, the close, and yesterday's close, so it can be checked on free daily data in seconds.

SPY and ES agree on this rule almost perfectly on the overlapping dates (daily P&L correlation 0.92; +10.2 vs +9.6 bp/day). Nothing in the intraday pipeline was needed to see it.

2025 is the best year of 28 for this rule, and April 2025 alone supplied 55% of that year. The ES sample sits on top of it.

  • Out of sample. SPY from 10 Apr to 2 Sep 2026: 100 days, 42% win, −10.0 bp/day, t = −1.8, negative in all six months.
  • The sample's holes flattered it. On the 43 dates missing from the ES sample inside its own window (holidays, IBKR gaps), the rule lost 12 bp/day.
  • Filters do not rescue it. A VIX ≥ 15 filter looks strong on ES (t = 3.3) and is nothing over 25 years (t = 0.3).
  • Literature. A 2026 systematic falsification study on MNQ futures reports the gap-fill fade failing at every tested entry time.
  • Also empty on this data: entry-time sweep 09:35–15:00, exit sweep, stop sweep (every stop lowers the mean), Nadex framing (nearest strike past the 10:00 price wins 44%), and the published first-half-hour-to-last-half-hour momentum relation (t = −0.6).

Two years of intraday data cannot tell a regime from an edge. Test every candidate on 25 years of daily data first.

Pipeline

Two data bugs to fix before trusting another run

  • 21 days were scored against the previous day's open. The 09:30 open is forward-filled in barsToCleaning.ipynb, so the 07:00–09:15 pre-market bars present for the first 22 days (Mar–Apr 2024) inherit the prior day's open, survive as non-equal rows, and become the day's first row in clean.csv. Fix: set the open per date, not by forward fill.
  • Define "initial direction" at a fixed clock time (09:35 or 10:00), not as "the first bar that differs from the open".
  • Bars after 10:00 are sampled roughly every 15 minutes, so any MAE/MFE built from bar highs and lows is a lower bound.
